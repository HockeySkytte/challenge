xG Models
Two simple expected-goal models, and how far to trust them
These models are built on very little data. The all-shots model learned from {{ "{:,}".format(models.models[0].rows) }} shots and {{ models.models[0].goals }} goals. Thirty goals spread across four inputs is not much for a model to work with, so the numbers below show it has picked up something real — not that it measures shot quality precisely. Small differences between players should not be read as meaningful.
How they are built

Expected goals puts a number between 0 and 1 on a shot: how likely it was to become a goal. Both models are plain logistic regressions, built with scikit-learn, and both look at only four things.

InputWhat it is
shot_distanceHow far the puck was from the net, in feet
shot_angleHow much of the goal mouth the shooter could see, in degrees
trafficWhether there was traffic in front of the shooter
one_timerWhether the shot came off a one-timer

The four inputs are measured in very different units, so they are standardised before fitting. Without that, the model's penalty would fall hardest on whichever input happens to be written in the largest numbers, which is distance.

Both are trained only on the international games at 5v5, and only those rows are given a value. The models say nothing about a college game or a power play — that is deliberate, rather than guessing at situations they were never built for.
