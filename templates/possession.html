What this shows

Every event on this page is the moment a player takes control of the puck. The number in each box is what the team that won the puck got out of the next 15 seconds - {{ metric }} for minus against - per possession. Blue is a positive value for that team, red is negative.

Dividing by the number of possessions is what makes the map comparable across the rink. Without it, the busiest parts would look the most extreme simply because more possessions started there, and the map would show where play happens rather than what a possession is worth.

The rink is mirrored, so the two halves of the map always match and every box is labelled - a merged or mirrored region carries the same number throughout.

Why the map looks coarse

There is not much data here: {{ "{:,}".format(windows) }} windows across the whole rink, from the international games at 5v5. Two things keep the map readable anyway.

  • The rink is mirrored across the centre line. The ice is symmetric left to right, so a box and its twin on the other side count as one region. That roughly doubles the data behind each part of the map. A box straddling the centre line is its own twin.
  • Thin regions are merged into their neighbours. A region with fewer than {{ minimum }} windows behind it is folded into whichever region is nearest, and that repeats until every region has enough to say something. The {{ regions|length }} regions on this map started as 36 mirrored pairs; {{ merged }} of them are now built from more than two boxes, and they are all in the quiet corners.

Even after that, these are small samples. Read the shape of the map rather than the exact numbers, and do not lean on any single region.
