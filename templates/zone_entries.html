What this shows

Every event on this page is a puck moved into the offensive zone. The number in each box is what the team that made the entry got out of the next 15 seconds — {{ metric }} for minus against — per entry. Blue is a positive value for that team, red is negative.

Why the entries are filed by type

A zone entry is about crossing the blue line, but the coordinate it is logged at is not where the entry happened. A carry or a play is recorded where the puck crossed the line, and a dump where the puck was released — well before the line. Read literally, the same kind of entry scatters across the neutral zone and the offensive zone.

So each entry is filed by its type instead, keeping only the third of the ice it was made from:

  • Carried and Played go in the first offensive column — O24, O25 or O26.
  • Dumped goes in the last neutral column — N01, N02 or N03.

The rest of the rink is left unfilled. Those boxes hold no entries by construction, so they are not empty regions — they are simply not where this question applies.

Reading it

Nothing is mirrored and nothing is merged on this page. Every box stands on its own, because the entries are filed by type rather than by coordinate: a carry up the left wall and a carry up the right wall are different things here, and folding them together would hide the comparison the page exists to make.

That does leave thin boxes. {{ "{:,}".format(entries) }} entries across {{ regions|length }} boxes means the quietest holds only {{ "{:,}".format(thinnest) }}, so read the difference between the columns rather than the detail within one. Hover any box to see how many entries are behind its number.
